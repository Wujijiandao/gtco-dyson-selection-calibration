# From Dyson-sphere candidates to population constraints
## Calibrating selection and real-image completeness in Gaia–2MASS–WISE searches

**Target journal:** MNRAS Paper  
**Status:** submission draft v1  
**Author/affiliation fields:** placeholders

## Abstract

Mid-infrared searches for partial Dyson spheres can identify sources whose broad-band spectral energy distributions are consistent with stellar obscuration plus thermal waste heat, but candidate counts are not occurrence rates unless the survey selection function is calibrated through the same decision operator that defines the candidate sample. We construct a real-data calibration framework using a 100-pc Gaia DR3 parent sample cross-matched to 2MASS and AllWISE, and quantify three coupled components of the selection response: photometric recovery, host-quality filtering, and image-level morphology.

A 10-band injection–recovery experiment using 265 real stellar templates and a 220,745-model partial-Dyson library gives a mean photometric recovery of 0.910 over a 54-cell grid in waste-heat temperature and covering factor. Applying Gaia/WISE host cuts at the source level to the same 3,000 validation stars yields a joint photometric-plus-host recovery of 0.414, compared with 0.402 from multiplying stage-averaged responses, demonstrating a measurable selection correlation.

We then inject co-spatial waste-heat point-source flux into real AllWISE W3/W4 intensity and uncertainty images. A leave-one-host-out analysis, in which each test star is excluded from both the empirical PSF and the calibration envelope, finds no morphology rejection among 23 independent baseline-clean hosts in either W3 or W4 within the tested unsaturated regime. The corresponding one-sided 95 per cent exact lower bound on host-level morphology retention is 0.878 in each band; the joint W3+W4 bound is 0.873 for 22 hosts.

We therefore separate image completeness into environmental, detector-response, and conditional morphology terms. Finally, a stellar population occurrence can only be inferred as

\[
f_{\rm DS}=\frac{\pi_{\rm DS}r_{\rm cand}}{C_{\rm DS}}
\]

when candidate rate, candidate composition, and completeness refer to the same selection operator. The present work calibrates key components of this relation but deliberately does not report a Dyson-sphere occurrence rate.
